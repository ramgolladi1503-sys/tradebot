## Why attractive near-misses were rejected

Several hypotheses showed locally attractive replication statistics. They were **not** promoted because none survived the campaign-wide multiplicity burden.

Examples include cross-scale, dispersion, and common-factor-residual motifs with positive replication means, hit rates and bootstrap intervals. Their adjusted q-values remained far above the frozen campaign threshold.

Promoting one of these after seeing 648 outcomes, or changing its threshold/window until it passes, would be precisely the overfitting behavior this campaign was designed to prevent.

Therefore:

- no near miss is a strategy;
- no failed family may be reopened on this corpus;
- no threshold or horizon may be tuned to rescue a failed result;
- the unopened tail may not be consumed merely to inspect a near miss.

## Reopen conditions

Historical discovery may reopen only when at least one materially new capability is independently certified before hypothesis evaluation, for example:

1. richer synchronized historical microstructure;
2. materially longer genuinely independent history unused by prior selection;
3. a materially different instrument universe with independently certified data;
4. a materially different quote-supported execution horizon;
5. an explicitly authorized different economic exposure with its own risk/cost contract.

The following are never sufficient alone:

- another indicator combination;
- a threshold grid;
- stop/target optimization;
- one favorable month or subgroup;
- renamed or lightly modified rejected mechanisms;
- promoting a local near miss that failed campaign-wide correction.
